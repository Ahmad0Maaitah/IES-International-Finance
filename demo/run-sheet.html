Run sheet: the 15 minutes

Hub, teaching approach, the seven-minute demonstration and the syllabus, in fifteen minutes. The same scripts live in the decks' speaker notes (press S).

Pre-flight, on the day

  • Open the hub in Chrome on the laptop that will drive the projector. Open Approach and the demo once so they are warm, then come back to the hub. F toggles full screen inside a deck.
  • Check The Economist's Big Mac page. If a newer edition than January 2026 is out, type the new US and London prices into the two boxes on the calculator slide; the deck uses US $6.12 and London £5.29.
  • Check today's dollar-pound rate. If it is far from $1.32, set the slider to it on the Heathrow and calculator slides; the big number updates itself.
  • Bright room: press the sun button in the toolbar once; the choice is remembered.
  • Reload the demo just before starting so rehearsal votes are cleared (or press Reset on the guess slide).
  • Fallback: everything runs on a phone held up to the room. Worst case, the whole demonstration is three divisions on a whiteboard.

The three numbers, in case the slide is not there

Purchasing power parity rate = US price divided by London price = 6.12 / 5.29 = $1.157 per pound. London burger in dollars at the market rate = 5.29 × 1.3249 = $7.01. Pound against parity = market rate divided by PPP rate minus one = 1.3249 / 1.157 − 1 = +14.5 per cent, overvalued. With the January 2026 market rate the index used ($1.338), the figure is +15.7 per cent, which is what The Economist published.

If the room asks

What if a student has never studied finance?
Nothing on this slide needs more than division. Weeks 1 and 2 build the shared language and the warm-up quizzes show me who needs the maths clinic in week 2. The course has no prerequisite by design.
Is this just a gimmick, or is it assessed?
The same calculation is a midterm problem, and "does the Big Mac index predict where the pound goes" is one of the research-paper topics. The guess and the vote are not graded; the ability to compute and interpret parity is.
What if the technology fails in class?
The deck runs on any phone and offline once opened, and every interaction has a hands-and-whiteboard version. I have taught the same sessions in rooms with no projector.
How do you grade participation fairly?
With a rubric: one five-minute news briefing per student, scheduled on a rota in week 1, plus a simple record of engagement in the activities. Attendance alone does not earn participation marks, and the rubric is on Moodle from day one.
What is your policy on AI?
Allowed for brainstorming, language and code help, disclosed in one line; the theory choice, data work and analysis must be the group's own, and both exams are closed book. Stated in week 1 with examples.
What do you need from IES?
A projector, Moodle access about three weeks before the term, and a timetable slot for the Bank of England Museum in week 3. Everything else is built.
Why the burger and not something more serious?
Because the room just understood purchasing power parity in four minutes. The serious version, the parity conditions with interest rates and the forward market, comes in the second half of the same session, once the idea is in the room.
